# Fasteners

`bitbybit.occt.parts.mechanical.fasteners` builds the bolts, screws, nuts and washers that hold a model together, at the sizes their standards give, and `bitbybit.engineering.mechanical.fasteners` gives the numbers behind them: the hole a bolt passes through, the counterbore a cap screw sinks into, how thick a washer is. [Standard parts](./intro) explains the standards, units, frames and roles they share.

## A bolted plate

Here an M8 bolt passes through a plate, with a washer and a nut underneath. The table gives the clearance hole, so the bolt passes freely, and the washer's thickness, so the nut sits right under it; in Rete and Blockly, `bitbybit.json.getValueOnProp` reads the `thickness` out of the table's answer:

- The normal clearance hole for M8 is 9 millimetres, so the 8 millimetre bolt stands clear of the plate.
- The bolt's frame is the plate's top face, its normal pointing up: the head rises above the plate and the shank runs down through it.
- The washer and the nut take frames under the plate with their normals pointing down, so each rises away from the plate. The washer is 1.6 millimetres thick, so the nut's frame sits 1.6 below the plate.
- A nut's hole is the thread's nominal diameter, so the bolt passes through the nut without overlapping it. The parts touch where they bear and overlap nowhere, which `bitbybit.occt.analysis.clashes.betweenShapes` confirms.

## What each standard holds

The metric parts are in `bitbybit.occt.parts.mechanical.fasteners.iso`, sized by M1.6 to M24:

- `hexBolt` is a hexagon head bolt threaded to the head, ISO 4017, or, with `fullyThreaded` false, threaded only for the length ISO 4014 gives, leaving a plain shank.
- `socketHeadCapScrew` is an ISO 4762 cap screw: a round head with a hexagon recess for a key.
- `hexNut` is an ISO 4032 hexagon nut, chamfered on both sides.
- `setScrew` is a set screw with a hexagon recess, threaded its whole length, with a `point` that bears on the part: flat (ISO 4026), cone (ISO 4027), dog (ISO 4028) or cup (ISO 4029).
- `washer` is an ISO 7089 plain washer, or with `chamfered` the ISO 7090 one, chamfered on the outside of the face away from its seat, from M5.

The inch parts are in `bitbybit.occt.parts.mechanical.fasteners.ansi`, sized by #0 to 1 inch, coarse (UNC) or fine (UNF) by `series`:

- `hexBolt` is an ASME B18.2.1 hex cap screw, threaded for the length the standard gives, or to the head with `fullyThreaded`.
- `socketHeadCapScrew` is an ASME B18.3 cap screw.
- `hexNut` is a B18.6.3 machine screw nut up to #12 and a B18.2.2 hex nut from 1/4 inch.
- `setScrew` is an ASME B18.3 set screw with a cup, flat, cone, oval or half dog point.
- `washer` is a B18.22.1 type A plain washer, narrow or wide by `series`, from #6.

`bitbybit.occt.parts.mechanical.fasteners.dowelPin` makes a cylindrical pin of any `diameter` and `length`, chamfered at each end after ISO 2338; it starts at its frame's origin and runs along the normal.

**The thread is a light face unless you ask for it.** A bolt or cap screw shows its threaded length as a face of its own, which draws and builds fast. With `modelledThread` on, the thread is cut as the [machining](./machining) functions cut one, every face of it named `thread`, which takes longer to build but is true to the part. A set screw sits with its recessed end on the frame's origin and runs against the normal, into the hole it fills.

## Roles

Each part has a twin that names its faces, as [Standard parts](./intro#roles-which-face-is-which) describes:

- `bitbybit.occt.parts.mechanical.fasteners.iso.hexBoltWithRoles` and `bitbybit.occt.parts.mechanical.fasteners.ansi.hexBoltWithRoles` name a bolt's `head`, `seat` (under the head), `shank`, `thread` and `end` (the tip); a bolt threaded to its head has no `shank`. Their `seat` frame is the frame you gave.
- `socketHeadCapScrewWithRoles`, in both standards, names the same roles and the `recess`.
- `hexNutWithRoles` names a nut's `seat` (its bearing face), `top`, `side` and `thread` (the hole).
- `setScrewWithRoles` names a set screw's `top` (the recessed end), `recess`, `thread` and `end` (the point).
- `washerWithRoles` names a washer's `seat`, `top`, `bore` and `rim`, an ISO 7090 washer's chamfer included in the `rim`.
- `bitbybit.occt.parts.mechanical.fasteners.dowelPinWithRoles` names a pin's `side` and its `end` faces, chamfers included.

## The tables

`bitbybit.engineering.mechanical.fasteners.iso` and `bitbybit.engineering.mechanical.fasteners.ansi` give the numbers the parts are built from, in `unit`:

- `clearanceHole` gives the diameter of the hole a screw passes through, `close`, `normal` or `loose` by `fit`: ISO 273 for metric screws, where M6 normal is 6.6 millimetres, and ASME B18.2.8 for inch ones, where 1/4 normal is 0.281 inches, a 9/32 drill.
- `hexBoltSizes` gives a bolt's width across flats, head height and threaded length for a `length`: an M8 bolt 40 long has 13 across flats, a 5.3 high head and 22 threaded.
- `socketHeadCapScrewSizes` gives a cap screw's head, its recess, its threaded length and the counterbore that sinks it below the surface: DIN 974-1 for metric screws, half a millimetre deeper than the head, and 0.02 inches deeper for inch ones.
- `hexNutSizes` gives a nut's width across flats and height: M8 is 13 across and 6.8 high.
- `setScrewSizes` gives a set screw's recess and its point; a short screw, by `length`, gets a shallower recess and, at its cone, a blunter angle.
- `washerSizes` gives a washer's inner and outer diameters and thickness, and `bitbybit.engineering.mechanical.fasteners.iso.chamferedWasherSizes` an ISO 7090 washer chamfered on one face, from M5.
