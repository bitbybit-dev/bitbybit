## This template: drone assembly

A multirotor drone as a CAD assembly: twenty-seven unique parts built once on the kernel and placed as instances by a tree of sub-assemblies, the way a STEP assembly is organised. The parameters are few on purpose - arm count (4, 6 or 8), arm length, propeller diameter and a finish - so the geometry can be rich: a lofted canopy with a honeycomb decal and a raised hatch with the logo cut through it over a contrasting inlay, flat carbon arms that widen toward the body with a row of hexagonal cutouts, plates with hexagonal lightening holes and a round opening where each leg's boss comes through, outrunner motors slotted so the copper stator shows, propellers lofted through twisted sections, a camera gimbal, a battery with straps, bent landing legs each ending in a spring damper (a revolved barrel and piston rod, a helix swept into a coil spring, a rubber pad lofted through circles), a GPS puck, antennas, standoffs and forty-plus socket-head bolts. The model stands on the ground; the viewer flies it.

- `src/parts.ts` builds each unique part in its own local frame: the arm along +X between the plates, the motor with its base at y = 0, the bolt with its head below y = 0 and the shank up. Every dimension is in `FRAME`, in millimetres. Several constructions are deliberate: the propeller's blades are fused into a domed hub tall enough to swallow their twisted roots and the junction is filleted, about a second once per diameter, and the counter-clockwise propeller is the clockwise one mirrored across the XY plane (`mirrored`), which costs nothing; the canopy is a loft of rounded rectangles because the kernel refuses fillets on lofted B-spline solids; every cutout (the plates' openings and honeycomb, the arms' hexagons, the logo through the hatch) is an inner wire of one face that is then extruded, never a boolean, which is why they cost milliseconds - through `faceWithHoles`, always, because the kernel adds an inner wire as it comes and only a wire running against the outline cuts a hole (one running with it is added as material; the logo's two wires run opposite ways); the emblem is an inlay a hair smaller than the hatch's cut, sitting below its surface, so the logo reads carved without cutting the canopy; and the honeycomb on the canopy is `subdivideToHexagonWires` on the side faces of a copy grown by a hair, wires the viewer draws as lines (projecting a flat grid onto the loft took twenty seconds). The logo is mirrored on load (`logoFaces`) so it reads the right way from above. The coil spring needs every helix field, `clockwise` and `tolerance` included: an unset tolerance gives a null wire the sweep then refuses.
- `src/model.ts` is the plan. `layout(params)` is pure: it returns the modules (airframe, one per arm, landing gear, payload), each lifted by the landing gear's height, and every instance with its placement, a list of matrices from the base package's `transforms` service applied first to last; the arm module is laid out once and turned by `rotationY(angle)`. The leg is a `definition` node - a sub-assembly laid out once in its own frame (tube, damper, spring, foot, two bolts) - and the landing gear holds four `reference` nodes that place it; `counts.instances` counts placements as the document expands them, so those six parts count four times. `buildParts` builds the parts through a `SPECS` table whose `key` says what a part depends on, so a previous model's parts are reused when the key is unchanged: the finish rebuilds nothing, the arm count rebuilds the arm (`armRootWidth` narrows its root so eight fit around the body), the arm length rebuilds one part. A part's `kind` is `solid` or `wires`; the honeycomb is the one wire part. `clampParams` limits the propeller so its tips clear the neighbours and the canopy (`maxPropDiameter`).
- `src/assembly.ts` turns a model into an assembly document (`occt.assembly.manager`: parts with colours, assembly nodes with matrices, instance nodes). A definition is built as a document of its own and brought in with `createImportedPart`, which is the one way to place a sub-assembly several times: an instance node naming an assembly node's id is silently dropped. `buildDocument` therefore returns the document with its source documents, and the viewer deletes them together. The bill of materials is read from `getAssemblyHierarchy`, which lists every placement the references expand to (`getDocumentParts` would count a part inside the leg once). `Z_UP` is the frame the STEP export applies to the top-level modules so CAD tools open it upright; it is a rotation, never a mirror. `deleteDocument` exists only on the worker, so the model modules never call it; `main.ts` does.
- `src/materials.ts` is one table for both runtimes: the viewer builds a `MeshPhysicalMaterial` per material, the document gets the same colour per part, and an instance can override its material (front LEDs green, rear red; the propellers are all in the accent). Each finish also names the emblem's inlay colour (white on Midnight, black on Arctic and Signal), and `edgeColour` picks white lines for dark surfaces and black for light or orange ones by luminance.
- `src/main.ts` draws each part once with its material and its edge colour (a prototype is keyed by geometry and edge colour, so a finish that flips a part from dark to light redraws that part's lines) and clones it per instance; the clones share geometry and material. Edges are on by default at `EDGE_WIDTH` pixels; the honeycomb is drawn as lines only. The rotor speed slider (rpm, 0 to 360) drives the propellers in the animation loop and the flight: the drone climbs toward `FLIGHT_CEILING` times the throttle, floats there with a slow bob and sway, and lands when the rotors stop; the model itself always stands on the ground, which is what the exports carry. Every line material gets a small negative polygon offset so edges and the honeycomb never flicker against the surfaces they lie on. A room environment and tone mapping serve the metals; exports build a document, write it and delete it with its sources. In the scene a definition is one template group and each reference a clone of it. `cameraOptions` gives the orbit camera explicit distance limits from the scene size and leaves its sensitivities at their base values: the engine's defaults scale zoom and pan by the scene size although both already follow the camera's distance, so a scene this large would jump a third of the way in per wheel notch.
- `scripts/smoke.ts` builds the default drone on the in-process kernel, fetches the emblem's logo from the CDN (skipped offline; the summary says `emblem: false`), and prints one JSON line, the only line on stdout because the kernel's own progress lines are silenced: `template`, `params`, `emblem`, `partsMs`, per-part `solids`, `faces` and `volume`, `layouts` for 4, 6 and 8 arms, the `bom`, `modules`, `instances`, `bolts`, `glbBytes`, `stepBytes`, `stepSolids` and the STEP's `envelope`. It exits 1 when a solid part is missing, invalid or empty, or the wire part has no edges; when the plate, the arm or the hatch holds the volume it would have with its holes uncut (the logo's cut is checked against the emblem's own area); when a layout's instance, bolt or module count is off, names an unknown part or parent, or lets propellers reach each other; when the document's parts, hierarchy or bill of materials disagree with the layout; when the GLB lacks its magic or the STEP its header; when the leg is not defined once and placed four times or the hierarchy's node count is not the root plus the modules, the references and the expanded placements; or when the STEP read back has a different number of solids, does not stand on the ground, does not top out at the propeller nuts, or reaches further than an arm plus a propeller radius.

Commands:

- `npm run dev` serves the viewer at http://localhost:5173
- `npm run build` typechecks and bundles for production; `npm run typecheck` and `npm run lint` run alone as well
- `npm run smoke` typechecks, lints, then builds the drone headlessly and prints the summary line. The smoke script types the in-process kernel as the same `Occt` the browser code sees, through one `inProcess` cast, and awaits every call, so `buildModel` runs exactly as it does in the browser and `scripts/` is typechecked with the rest. The two loaders that exist only in-process (`io.loadSTEPorIGES` with `filetext`, for the logo and the STEP round trip) are called on the service itself and their result crosses back through `asShape`.

To add a part: write its function in `parts.ts` in a local frame, add a `SPECS` row in `model.ts` with a key naming what it depends on, place it in `layout` with `placed(at, rotation)`, give it a material, and run the smoke: the instance count assertions there are `38 + 15 * arms` plus the emblem and will need the new count.

Units are millimetres everywhere. A placement is a list of column-major 4x4 matrices applied first to last, the form the assembly manager, `point.transformPoint` and the `transforms` service of `@bitbybit-dev/base` all share; `kernel.ts` constructs that service once (`transforms`) and nothing here multiplies a matrix by hand. `layout` names its own vocabulary over it: `move(to)` is `translationXYZ`, `turnX` and `turnY` are `rotationCenterX` and `rotationCenterY` about the origin (right-hand turns, so `turnX(90)` sends +Y to +Z and `turnY(90)` sends +X to -Z), and `placed(at, rotation)` turns first, then moves. Three.js composes a placement with `Matrix4.premultiply`, so one list feeds the document and the scene alike. The kernel is loaded in-process from `node_modules`, the same WebAssembly the browser runs inside a worker; its content-hashed `.wasm` is found beside the loader rather than spelled out. Bounding boxes of B-spline solids are loose in OpenCascade, which is why the smoke proves placements by counting solids in a STEP round trip and keeps its extents checks generous.
